1. Two Different Documents, Two Different Purposes

It's a common mix-up: many new arrivals in the UK assume their National Insurance Number and their Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) are the same thing, since both feel like official proof you're allowed to be here. In reality, they serve completely different purposes, and most visa holders need to deal with both at some point.

2. What Is a National Insurance Number?

Your National Insurance Number is a unique reference used by HMRC to track your tax and National Insurance contributions throughout your working life in the UK. It follows a set format of two letters, six numbers and a final letter, for example AB123456C, and it stays with you permanently, regardless of how many jobs or visas you have over the years.

You apply for it separately from your visa — see our guide on how to apply for a National Insurance Number online.

3. What Is a Biometric Residence Permit?

A BRP is a physical card that used to confirm a person's immigration status, right to work or study, and access to public services in the UK. It includes your photo, fingerprints on record, and visa conditions. Since the UK has moved towards digital immigration status, most people are now issued an eVisa instead of a physical BRP card.

5. Do You Need Both?

If you're a visa holder working or studying in the UK, you'll typically need both. Your BRP or eVisa confirms your right to be in the UK and to work or study, while your National Insurance Number is what allows you to be paid correctly and pay the right amount of tax. Neither document replaces the other, and you'll usually be asked to provide details from both when applying for a NINO — see our full documents checklist for a NINO application.

Quick way to remember: Your BRP or eVisa answers "am I allowed to be here?" while your National Insurance Number answers "how is my income taxed and recorded?"

6. The Shift to eVisas

The UK has been phasing out physical BRP cards in favour of online, digital immigration status known as an eVisa. If you have an eVisa rather than a physical card, you'll use a share code to prove your status instead of a card number when applying for your National Insurance Number.

You can check your own eVisa status directly through GOV.UK's Biometric Residence Permits page, which explains the transition in more detail.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a BRP the same as a National Insurance Number? +
No, they are different documents. A BRP confirms your immigration status, while a National Insurance Number is used to track your tax and contributions.
Do I need a BRP to apply for a National Insurance Number? +
In many cases yes, since your BRP or eVisa details help confirm your right to work or study when applying for your number.
Does my National Insurance Number expire like my BRP? +
No, your National Insurance Number is permanent, while your BRP or eVisa status is tied to the length of your visa.
Can I have a National Insurance Number without a BRP? +
Yes, UK and Irish citizens typically don't need a BRP at all, but still have a National Insurance Number.
What replaced the physical BRP card? +
The UK has been moving towards eVisas, a digital record of immigration status accessed online instead of a physical card.
Which document do I show my employer? +
Your BRP or eVisa proves your right to work, while your National Insurance Number is used separately for payroll and tax purposes.
Do both documents use the same reference format? +
No, a National Insurance Number follows a letters-and-numbers format, while a BRP has its own separate document number.
Who issues each document? +
The Home Office issues BRPs and eVisas, while HMRC issues and manages National Insurance Numbers.
Can I rent a flat using just my National Insurance Number? +
No, landlords typically require proof of immigration status such as a BRP or eVisa, not your National Insurance Number.
