Do You Need a National Insurance Number to Work in the UK?

Starting a new job but haven't received your National Insurance Number yet? Here's what UK law actually requires, and what to tell your employer.

1. The Short Answer

A lot of new arrivals in the UK assume they cannot start work until their number is in hand, but that's not quite right. You do not need a National Insurance Number to start a job — what you actually need is the legal right to work in the UK. Your National Insurance Number is something you apply for and provide once you're already working.

If you haven't applied yet, it's worth starting the process straight away — see our guide on how to apply for a National Insurance Number online.

2. What You Actually Need to Start Work

To be legally employed in the UK, your employer needs evidence of your right to work — not your National Insurance Number. This might be your passport, visa, BRP, or share code from an eVisa.

  • Proof of your right to work (passport, visa, BRP or share code)
  • Your bank details, for payroll purposes
  • A completed starter checklist (formerly known as a P46) if you don't yet have a P45

Your National Insurance Number is not a legal requirement to begin employment, though you should apply as soon as possible and pass it on once received.

3. What Happens on Payroll Without a Number

If you start work before your National Insurance Number arrives, your employer will usually place you on a temporary tax code and continue paying you as normal. Once you provide your number, your payroll and tax records are simply updated to reflect it — nothing about your pay is lost or delayed as a result.

Note: Your employer may ask you to complete a "starter checklist" confirming you've applied for a National Insurance Number, which helps them apply the correct temporary tax code.

5. What to Tell Your Employer

Be upfront with your employer that you've applied for your National Insurance Number and are waiting for it to arrive. Most UK employers are familiar with this situation, particularly for new arrivals, international students, and Skilled Worker visa holders, and will simply update their payroll system once your number comes through.

You can read more about how to speed this along in our guide to how long a NINO application takes.

6. When You Should Have Applied

Ideally, you should apply for your National Insurance Number as soon as you arrive in the UK and know you'll be working or studying — there's no need to wait for a confirmed start date. Applying early avoids any awkward conversations with payroll later on.

For official confirmation of employment and National Insurance rules, GOV.UK's National Insurance page is the definitive source, and ACAS offers free advice if you run into any issues with your employer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need a National Insurance Number to work in the UK? +
No, you can legally start work without one as long as you have the right to work in the UK. Your National Insurance Number is applied for separately.
Can I be paid without a National Insurance Number? +
Yes, your employer can pay you using a temporary tax code until your number arrives, then update their records.
What do I give my employer if I don't have a number yet? +
You should provide proof of your right to work, such as your passport, visa, BRP or eVisa share code, along with a completed starter checklist.
Will I be taxed more without a National Insurance Number? +
You may be placed on a temporary tax code initially, but this is usually corrected once your number is provided.
How soon should I apply once I start working? +
As soon as possible — ideally as soon as you arrive in the UK, rather than waiting until your first day of work.
Does my employer need my number before my first payday? +
Not necessarily, though providing it as early as possible helps ensure your payroll and tax records are accurate.
Can I work multiple jobs without a National Insurance Number? +
Yes, the same principle applies across multiple employers, though it's best to apply for your number promptly regardless.
Is a National Insurance Number the same as right to work? +
No, they are separate. Right to work confirms your legal status; the National Insurance Number is for tax and benefits records.
What if my employer refuses to let me start without a number? +
This isn't a legal requirement on their part; you may want to point them to official GOV.UK guidance or seek advice from ACAS.
