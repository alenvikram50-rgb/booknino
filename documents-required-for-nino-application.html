1. Why Documents Matter for Your NINO Application

The documents required for a NINO application are the single biggest reason applications get delayed or bounced back. HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) needs to confirm who you are and that you have the right to work or study in the UK before a National Insurance Number can be issued, so every document you submit is checked closely against your online form.

Getting your paperwork right the first time is the easiest way to avoid weeks of back-and-forth. Before you start your new National Insurance Number application, it helps to know exactly what you'll be asked for and why.

2. Core Documents Required for a NINO Application

Most applicants need to provide at least one primary identity document and, in many cases, a secondary supporting document. The exact combination depends on your nationality and immigration status, but the following are the documents you'll be asked for most often:

  • A valid passport — UK or foreign, current and not expired
  • Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) — for non-UK nationals with a visa
  • Home Office share code — if you have digital immigration status (eVisa)
  • Birth or adoption certificate — for UK-born applicants without a passport
  • Marriage or civil partnership certificate — if your name has changed

You can check the full official list of accepted documents on GOV.UK, which is updated whenever HMRC changes its identity verification rules.

3. Documents by Immigration Status

The documents you need for your NINO application will vary depending on how you came to be in the UK:

UK and Irish Citizens

A current passport is usually enough on its own. If you don't hold a passport, a birth certificate plus a second form of ID (such as a driving licence) is normally accepted.

Skilled Worker and Points-Based Visa Holders

You'll typically need your passport, your visa vignette or BRP, and your Home Office reference number. Read our dedicated guide on National Insurance Numbers for Skilled Worker visa holders for a full breakdown.

Students

International students should have their Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) number, passport and student visa details ready. Our guide to National Insurance Numbers for international students covers this in more depth.

EU Settlement Scheme Applicants

You'll generally need your eVisa share code rather than a physical card, since most EU Settlement Scheme status is now held digitally.

4. Proof of Address and Supporting Evidence

Alongside identity documents, you may be asked to confirm your current UK address. Acceptable proof usually includes:

  • A recent tenancy agreement or letter from your landlord
  • A utility bill dated within the last three months
  • A bank statement showing your name and UK address
  • A letter from your employer or university confirming your address

If your identity can't be confirmed digitally from the documents you upload, HMRC may invite you to an identity-check interview. You can read more about what to expect in our National Insurance Number interview guide.

6. Common Document Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expired documents — always check the expiry date before uploading
  • Mismatched names — if your name has changed, include the certificate showing the change
  • Cropped or partial photos — all four corners of the document should be visible
  • Old address details — proof of address should be recent and match your application
  • Missing Home Office reference — visa holders often forget this crucial number

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for a NINO application? +
The documents required for a NINO application usually include a valid passport or BRP, proof of your right to work or study in the UK, and sometimes proof of address. The exact list depends on your immigration status.
Can I apply for a National Insurance Number without a passport? +
Yes. UK-born applicants without a passport can often use a birth certificate alongside a second form of identification, such as a driving licence.
Do I need proof of address for a NINO application? +
In many cases yes. A recent utility bill, tenancy agreement or bank statement showing your UK address is commonly requested.
What if my documents are in a different name? +
You should include a marriage certificate, deed poll or other legal document that clearly links your previous name to your current one.
Can I use a photo of my documents instead of the originals? +
Yes, for the online application you upload clear photographs of your original documents rather than posting them.
What happens if my documents are rejected? +
HMRC will usually contact you to request clearer copies or additional evidence, which can add one to two weeks to your processing time.
Do skilled worker visa holders need extra documents? +
Yes, alongside your passport and BRP you'll usually need your Home Office reference or Certificate of Sponsorship number.
Is a driving licence enough on its own? +
Not usually. A UK driving licence is often accepted as a secondary document but is rarely enough as the sole form of identification.
How long should my proof of address be? +
Most proof-of-address documents should be dated within the last three months to be accepted.
