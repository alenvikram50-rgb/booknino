1. Before You Apply for a National Insurance Number

Knowing how to apply for a National Insurance Number online can save you a great deal of hassle, particularly if you're starting a new job or course in the UK. Before you begin, it's worth checking that you actually meet the National Insurance Number eligibility criteria and gathering the documents required for your application.

You must be living in the UK and have the right to work or study here. You do not need to have a job offer in hand before you apply, though many people apply once they've accepted a role.

2. Step-by-Step Online Application Process

The National Insurance Number online application is completed entirely through a digital form, with no need to visit a Jobcentre unless HMRC specifically asks you to attend an interview.

  1. Check eligibility — confirm you have the right to live and work or study in the UK
  2. Complete the online form — enter your personal details, current address and immigration status
  3. Upload your documents — clear photographs of your passport, BRP or other accepted ID
  4. Verify your identity — this may happen automatically or require a short video call or interview
  5. Submit and wait for confirmation — you'll receive an email confirming your application has been received

Once submitted, HMRC processes the application in the background — you can read our full breakdown of how long a NINO application takes to set expectations.

4. Identity Verification Explained

HMRC will try to verify your identity digitally first, cross-checking your details against Home Office and passport records. If this succeeds, you won't need to do anything else. If it doesn't, you may be invited to an identity-check interview — our National Insurance Number interview guide explains exactly what happens at these appointments.

Digital verification is usually faster for UK and Irish citizens with a valid passport, while some visa holders may find the process takes a little longer simply because there's more information to check.

5. After You Submit Your Application

Once your National Insurance Number application has been submitted, you'll receive a confirmation email with a reference number. Keep this safe, as you may need it if you contact HMRC or NI NO for an update.

  • You can start work before your number arrives, as long as you have the right to work
  • Tell your employer you've applied and provide your number as soon as it arrives
  • Your NI Number letter will be posted to the UK address you provided

6. Tips for a Smooth Online Application

Clear Photos

Upload sharp, well-lit images of every document, with all edges visible.

Accurate Address

Double-check your UK address, since your NI Number letter is posted there.

Apply Early

Submit your application as soon as you arrive rather than waiting until your first payday.

Check Your Inbox

HMRC may email you if further information is needed, so check your inbox and spam folder regularly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I apply for a National Insurance Number online? +
You complete an online form with your personal details, upload photographs of your identity documents, and go through identity verification, which may be automatic or involve a short interview.
Do I need a UK address to apply online? +
Yes, you need a current UK address, since this is where your National Insurance Number letter will be posted once it's issued.
Can I apply before I have a job offer? +
Yes, you can apply as soon as you have the right to live and work or study in the UK, even without a confirmed job.
Is the online application free to complete? +
Applying directly through HMRC's official process does not involve a government fee to submit your application.
What happens after I submit the online form? +
You'll receive a confirmation email, and HMRC will begin processing your application, which may include an identity verification step.
Can I edit my application after submitting it? +
Once submitted, you generally cannot edit the form yourself; you would need to contact HMRC directly to correct any details.
How long does the online form take to complete? +
Most applicants complete the form itself in around 15 to 30 minutes, though gathering documents beforehand can take longer.
Will I be asked to attend an interview? +
Only if your identity cannot be verified digitally. In that case, HMRC will invite you to a short identity-check interview.
Can someone apply on my behalf? +
In most cases you need to complete your own application, though a support service such as NI NO can guide you through each step.
