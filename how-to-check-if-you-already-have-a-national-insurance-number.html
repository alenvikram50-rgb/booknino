1. Why This Check Matters

Submitting a brand-new National Insurance Number application when you already have one on record can cause confusion, duplicate records, and unnecessary delays. Before you start a new application, it's worth taking a few minutes to check whether you were issued a number in the past, particularly if you've studied, worked, or claimed benefits in the UK before.

2. Signs You May Already Have a Number

  • You lived in the UK as a child, even briefly
  • You previously held a part-time job, internship or apprenticeship in the UK
  • You claimed Child Benefit, Jobseeker's Allowance, or a similar benefit in the past
  • You attended a UK university and worked part-time during your studies
  • A parent or guardian applied for you automatically around your 16th birthday

If any of the above sound familiar, it's worth checking thoroughly before assuming you need to apply from scratch.

3. How to Check Online

The quickest way to check is through your Personal Tax Account on GOV.UK. If you've ever registered for online tax services, your National Insurance Number will usually be displayed once you log in with your Government Gateway details.

  1. Go to your Personal Tax Account via GOV.UK
  2. Sign in, or create an account if you haven't before
  3. Check the "National Insurance" section of your account summary

You can find the official sign-in page on GOV.UK's National Insurance section.

4. How to Check Offline

If you don't have online access, you can check through paperwork you may already have:

  • Old payslips, P45s or P60s from previous UK employment
  • Letters from HMRC or the Department for Work and Pensions
  • Your Biometric Residence Permit, which sometimes displays the number
  • Calling HMRC's National Insurance helpline directly to confirm

If you can't find anything and believe you may have lost your details entirely, see our guide on what to do if you lose your National Insurance Number.

6. What to Do If You Confirm You Already Have One

If you discover you already have a National Insurance Number, there's no need to apply again — simply use your existing number going forward. If your personal details, such as your name or address, have changed since it was issued, you may want to update your details with HMRC rather than starting a fresh application.

If you're still unsure after checking, our team at NI NO can help confirm your status before you go ahead with a new application, so you avoid submitting a duplicate by mistake.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if I already have a National Insurance Number? +
The quickest way is through your Personal Tax Account on GOV.UK, which displays your number if one has already been issued to you.
What if I lived in the UK as a child? +
You may already have a National Insurance Number issued automatically around your 16th birthday, even if you've since moved abroad.
Can old payslips show my National Insurance Number? +
Yes, most UK payslips, P45s and P60s display your National Insurance Number if you've worked here before.
What happens if I apply again by mistake? +
It can create confusion in HMRC's records and may delay processing, so it's best to check thoroughly before submitting a new application.
Does my BRP show my National Insurance Number? +
Some Biometric Residence Permits display it, though this isn't guaranteed for every applicant.
Can I call HMRC to check? +
Yes, HMRC's National Insurance helpline can help confirm whether a number has already been issued to you.
What if my details have changed since my number was issued? +
You should update your details with HMRC rather than applying for a brand-new number, since your original number stays valid.
Is checking online free? +
Yes, checking your Personal Tax Account through GOV.UK does not involve any government charge.
Who can help me confirm my status if I'm unsure? +
You can contact HMRC directly, or reach out to a support service such as NI NO for guided help.
