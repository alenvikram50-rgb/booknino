1. How to Check Your National Insurance Contributions Online

Knowing how to check your National Insurance contributions online is essential for anyone working in the UK. Your National Insurance record tracks every contribution you have made throughout your working life, and it directly affects your eligibility for the State Pension and certain benefits.

The good news is that checking your National Insurance contributions online is quick, free, and straightforward. HMRC provides two main ways to view your National Insurance contributions:

  1. Online through the Government Gateway — using the "Check your National Insurance record" service on GOV.UK
  2. Through the HMRC app — available for iOS and Android devices

Both methods allow you to check your NI contributions, see your National Insurance contribution history, and find out if you have any gaps in your record that could affect your State Pension.

In this comprehensive guide, we will walk you through exactly how to check your National Insurance contributions online, what information you can access, and how to understand your National Insurance record.

2. What is a National Insurance Record?

Before we dive into how to check your National Insurance contributions online, it is important to understand what your National Insurance record actually is.

Your National Insurance record is a complete history of:

  • National Insurance contributions you have paid through employment (Class 1)
  • National Insurance contributions you have paid as a self-employed person (Class 2 and Class 4)
  • National Insurance credits you have received (for example, if you were caring for children or claiming certain benefits)
  • Any gaps in your contributions where you did not pay enough in a tax year
  • Your qualifying years towards the State Pension

Your National Insurance record is maintained by HMRC and is linked to your National Insurance number. Every time you work and pay National Insurance, your record is updated.

Why does this matter? Your National Insurance record determines how much State Pension you will receive when you retire, whether you are eligible for certain benefits, and if you need to pay voluntary contributions to fill gaps in your record.

3. Why You Should Check Your National Insurance Contributions

There are several compelling reasons to check your National Insurance contributions online:

  • Ensure Your Record is Accurate — Mistakes can happen. Your employer might not have reported your earnings correctly, or there might be an error in your National Insurance contributions history. Checking your record allows you to spot and correct any errors.
  • Check Your State Pension Forecast — When you view your National Insurance contributions, you can also see a forecast of your State Pension. This shows you how much you are on track to receive and whether you need to make additional contributions.
  • Identify Gaps in Your Contributions — If you have gaps in your National Insurance record, some years may not count towards your State Pension. By checking your record, you can identify these gaps and decide whether to fill them with voluntary contributions.
  • Plan for Your Retirement — Your National Insurance contributions directly affect your retirement income. By checking your NI contributions regularly, you can plan ahead and make sure you are on track for the State Pension you want.

4. How to Check Your National Insurance Contributions Online Using Government Gateway

The most common way to check your National Insurance contributions online is through the Government Gateway on the official GOV.UK website. Here is a step-by-step guide:

Step 1: Go to the Official Website

Visit the "Check your National Insurance record" page on GOV.UK at www.gov.uk/check-national-insurance-record.

Step 2: Sign In or Create a Government Gateway Account

You will need to sign in to check your National Insurance record. If you do not already have a Government Gateway account, you can create one during the process.

To create an account, you will need your National Insurance number, your personal details (name, date of birth, address), and an email address and phone number.

Step 3: Verify Your Identity

You will be told when you sign in if you need to prove your identity. This is to keep your details safe and normally involves using photo ID like a passport or driving licence.

Step 4: View Your National Insurance Record

Once you are signed in, you can view your National Insurance contributions for the current tax year and previous years. The information available includes:

  • What you have paid, up to the start of the current tax year (6 April 2025)
  • Any National Insurance credits you have received
  • Gaps in your contributions or credits
  • Whether you would benefit from paying voluntary contributions
  • How your State Pension forecast will change if you pay voluntary contributions
  • Whether you can pay voluntary contributions online and how much this will cost

Step 5: Review Your Information

Take time to review your National Insurance contributions history carefully. Look for any years where contributions are missing or where you have received credits.

5. How to Check Your National Insurance Record Using the HMRC App

The HMRC app is another excellent way to check your National Insurance contributions online. More than 5.6 million people have used the app since April 2025, making it a popular choice.

Step 1: Download the HMRC App

You can download the HMRC app from Google Play for Android devices or the Apple Store for iPhones and iPads.

Step 2: Sign In

Open the app and enter your Government Gateway user ID and password to sign in. If you do not have a user ID, you can create one within the app.

Step 3: Access Your National Insurance Record

Once signed in, you can view your National Insurance number, check your National Insurance contributions, check for gaps, view your State Pension forecast, and view your income and employment history from the past five years.

Step 4: Save or Share Your Information

The app allows you to save your National Insurance number to a digital wallet and opt for electronic communications from HMRC.

Benefits of Using the HMRC App

The HMRC app is convenient because you can check your National Insurance contributions anytime, anywhere, from your mobile device. It is particularly useful for quick checks of your NI payment history, viewing your State Pension forecast on the go, and checking for National Insurance contribution gaps.

6. What You Can See When You Check Your National Insurance Record

When you check your National Insurance record online, you will see a comprehensive overview of your National Insurance contributions history:

Contributions Paid

  • What you have paid, up to the start of the current tax year (6 April 2025)
  • Class 1 contributions from employment
  • Class 2 and Class 4 contributions from self-employment

National Insurance Credits

  • Caring for children under 12 (Child Benefit)
  • Caring for a sick or disabled person (Carer's Credit)
  • Claiming certain benefits (e.g., Jobseeker's Allowance, Employment and Support Allowance)

Qualifying Years

  • Which years count towards your State Pension
  • To get the full State Pension, you typically need 35 qualifying years

Gaps in Your Record

  • "Non-qualifying years" where you did not pay enough contributions
  • Whether you would benefit from paying voluntary contributions

State Pension Forecast

  • A forecast of your State Pension based on your current contributions

7. How to Check Your NI Contributions Without a Government Gateway Account

If you do not have a Government Gateway account and cannot create one, there are still ways to check your National Insurance contributions:

Request a Printed National Insurance Statement

You can request a printed National Insurance statement. You will need to say which years you want your statement to cover. You cannot request statements for the current or previous tax year.

Write to HMRC

You can write to HMRC to request a statement of all the National Insurance contributions recorded for you.

Address: PT Operations North East England, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1AN

Call the National Insurance Helpline

You can call the National Insurance helpline on 0300 200 3500 (from the UK) to request a statement. Opening times are Monday to Friday, 8am to 6pm, closed bank holidays.

Use the HMRC Digital Assistant

You can use HMRC's digital assistant to find information about checking your contributions. If the digital assistant cannot help, you can ask to transfer to an HMRC adviser if they are available.

8. How to Check Your National Insurance Contributions History

How to check your National Insurance contributions history is a common question. Here is how to access your full history:

Online Through Government Gateway

When you check your National Insurance record online, you can see your entire National Insurance contributions history. The record shows contributions for each tax year, starting from the beginning of your working life.

What Your History Shows

  • Each tax year and the contributions you made
  • Whether you received National Insurance credits
  • Whether the year counts as a qualifying year for your State Pension
  • Any gaps in your contributions

How Far Back Can You Check?

You can check your National Insurance contributions history for as long as you have been paying contributions. The online service shows contributions up to the start of the current tax year.

Understanding Your History

  • Full years — years where you paid enough contributions or received enough credits
  • Partial years — years where you did not pay enough
  • Missing years — years where you did not contribute at all

9. How to Check If Your NI Contributions Are Up to Date

How to check if your NI contributions are up to date is important for ensuring your State Pension is on track.

Check Your Record Online

The best way to check if your NI contributions are up to date is to view your National Insurance record online through the Government Gateway or HMRC app. Your record will show contributions up to the start of the current tax year (6 April 2025).

Look for Gaps

When you check your National Insurance contributions online, look for gaps or "non-qualifying years." These are years where you did not pay enough contributions or receive enough credits.

What to Do If Your Contributions Are Not Up to Date

If you find gaps in your National Insurance record, you may be able to pay voluntary contributions to fill them. The online service will tell you whether you would benefit from paying voluntary contributions, how your State Pension forecast will change if you pay, and whether you can pay online and how much this will cost.

Stay on Track

Checking your National Insurance contributions regularly helps you stay on track for the State Pension you want. It is recommended to check your NI contributions at least once a year.

10. How to Find Missing National Insurance Contributions

How to find missing National Insurance contributions is a common concern for many people.

Check Your Record Online

The first step is to check your National Insurance contributions online through the Government Gateway or HMRC app. Your record will show any gaps or missing contributions.

Why Contributions Might Be Missing

  • Your employer did not report your earnings correctly
  • You were self-employed and did not pay enough contributions
  • You took time off work and did not receive credits
  • There was an error in your records

What to Do If You Find Missing Contributions

If you find missing contributions in your National Insurance contributions history, you can:

  1. Contact HMRC — Call the National Insurance helpline on 0300 200 3500 to discuss your record
  2. Request a statement — Ask for a printed National Insurance statement to verify your contributions
  3. Pay voluntary contributions — If you have gaps, you may be able to pay voluntary contributions to fill them
  4. Check your employment history — Ensure all your employers have reported your earnings correctly

How to Fill Gaps

You can pay voluntary contributions (Class 3) to fill gaps in your National Insurance record. The online service will tell you whether you can pay online and how much it will cost.

11. How to Download Your National Insurance Statement

How to download your National Insurance statement is a useful skill for keeping a personal record of your contributions.

Download from Your Personal Tax Account

When you check your National Insurance contributions online through the Government Gateway, you can save or print your record. Your personal tax account allows you to view and save your National Insurance information.

Using the HMRC App

The HMRC app allows you to save your National Insurance number to a digital wallet. You can also access and save your National Insurance information within the app.

Request a Printed Statement

If you prefer a physical copy, you can request a printed National Insurance statement. You will need to say which years you want your statement to cover. You cannot request statements for the current or previous tax year.

Write to HMRC

You can write to HMRC to request a statement of all the National Insurance contributions recorded for you.

Address: PT Operations North East England, HM Revenue and Customs, BX9 1AN

If you have lost your National Insurance Number, you can request a replacement.

12. How to Check Your State Pension Record Alongside Your NI Contributions

When you check your National Insurance contributions online, you can also check your State Pension record.

State Pension Forecast

The online service provides a forecast of your State Pension based on your current contributions. This shows how much State Pension you could get, when you can get it, and if you can increase it.

Qualifying Years

The service also shows your qualifying years. To get the full State Pension, you typically need 35 qualifying years. If you have fewer than 35 qualifying years, you will receive a reduced State Pension.

How Voluntary Contributions Affect Your State Pension

If you decide to pay voluntary contributions to fill gaps in your record, the service will show you how your State Pension forecast will change.

Check Your State Pension Forecast

You can also use the dedicated "Check your State Pension forecast" service on GOV.UK. This gives you a detailed forecast of your State Pension.
