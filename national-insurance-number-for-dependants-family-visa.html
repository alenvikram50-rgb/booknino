1. National Insurance Number for Dependants & Family Visa Holders

If you are a dependant or a family visa holder in the UK, you may be wondering whether you need a National Insurance Number. A National Insurance Number for dependants and family visa holders is essential for anyone who wants to work, pay tax, or claim benefits in the UK.

A National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) is a unique reference number used by HMRC to identify you within the UK's tax and benefits system. For dependants and family visa holders, having a National Insurance Number is important for building your National Insurance contributions record, which determines your eligibility for the State Pension and other state benefits.

Whether you are a spouse, partner, child, or other dependant of a UK resident or visa holder, understanding your eligibility for a National Insurance Number is essential for your life in the UK. In this comprehensive guide, we will explain everything you need to know about the National Insurance Number for family visa holders, including who is eligible, how to apply, and what you need to do once you have your number.

2. Who are Dependants and Family Visa Holders?

Before we dive into the specifics of the National Insurance Number for dependants, let us first understand who qualifies as a dependant or family visa holder.

Dependants

A dependant is someone who relies on another person for financial support. In the context of UK immigration, dependants typically include:

  • Spouses and civil partners — Married partners or civil partners of UK residents or visa holders
  • Unmarried partners — Partners who have been living together in a relationship akin to marriage for at least two years
  • Children — Children under 18 years old (or over 18 in some circumstances)
  • Other family members — Parents, grandparents, or other relatives in exceptional circumstances

Family Visa Holders

Family visa holders are individuals who have been granted a visa to join their family member in the UK. The main types of family visas include:

  • Spouse Visa — For partners of British citizens or settled persons
  • Parent Visa — For parents of British citizens or settled children
  • Child Visa — For children of British citizens or settled persons
  • Family Visa (other dependants) — For other family members in exceptional circumstances

What This Means for National Insurance

As a dependant or family visa holder, you may be eligible to work in the UK. If you plan to work, you will need a National Insurance Number. Even if you do not plan to work immediately, it is a good idea to apply for one, as you may need it in the future for benefits or pension purposes.

3. Can Dependants Get a National Insurance Number in the UK?

Can dependants get a National Insurance Number in the UK? The short answer is yes — but there are conditions.

Eligibility for Dependants

Dependants and family visa holders can apply for a National Insurance Number if they meet the following criteria:

  • Age — You must be 16 or over
  • Residency — You must be living in the UK
  • Right to work — You must have the right to work in the UK (your visa or immigration status determines this)
  • Identity documents — You must be able to prove your identity

Important Note About Right to Work

Your right to work in the UK depends on the type of visa you hold. Some family visas allow you to work without restriction, while others may have restrictions. Before applying for a National Insurance Number, you should check your visa conditions.

Children Under 16

Children under 16 cannot apply for a National Insurance Number. They will be automatically assigned one when they turn 16. If a child under 16 needs an NI Number for a specific reason (such as working in a family business), you may need to contact HMRC directly.

What If You Are Not Allowed to Work?

If your family visa does not allow you to work in the UK, you cannot apply for a National Insurance Number. You can only apply if you have the right to work.

4. Do Family Visa Holders Need a National Insurance Number?

Do family visa holders need a National Insurance Number? The answer depends on your circumstances.

You Need an NI Number If:

  • You plan to work in the UK (either employed or self-employed)
  • You need to pay tax in the UK
  • You want to claim benefits in the UK
  • You want to build your State Pension entitlement
  • You want to open a Personal Tax Account with HMRC

You May Not Need an NI Number If:

  • You do not plan to work in the UK
  • You do not plan to claim benefits
  • You are not paying UK tax
  • You are only visiting the UK temporarily

Should You Apply Even If You Do Not Need It?

Even if you do not plan to work immediately, it is a good idea to apply for a National Insurance Number as soon as you are eligible. Having an NI Number ready means you will not have to wait if you decide to work or claim benefits in the future.

5. National Insurance Number for Spouse of UK Citizen

The National Insurance Number for spouse of UK citizen is an important topic for spouses who have moved to the UK.

Eligibility for Spouses

If you are the spouse of a UK citizen or a settled person in the UK, and you have been granted a Spouse Visa, you are generally eligible to apply for a National Insurance Number.

Right to Work

Most Spouse Visas allow you to work in the UK without restriction. This means you can apply for a National Insurance Number and start working as soon as you have the right to work.

When to Apply

You should apply for your National Insurance Number as soon as possible after arriving in the UK. You do not need to have a job offer before applying.

What If You Have a BRP?

If you have a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), your National Insurance Number may already be printed on the back. Always check your BRP before applying.

6. National Insurance Number for Children of UK Residents

The National Insurance Number for children of UK residents is important for families with children.

Children Under 16

Children under 16 do not need a National Insurance Number. They will be automatically assigned one when they turn 16. The number will be sent to them by post around their 16th birthday.

Children Over 16

Once a child turns 16, they can apply for a National Insurance Number if they plan to work or claim benefits. They can apply online through the official GOV.UK website.

Children with BRP

If the child has a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), their National Insurance Number may already be printed on the back. Check the BRP before applying.

Working as a Child

Children under 16 can work in the UK under certain conditions (such as part-time work or work in a family business). However, they do not need a National Insurance Number for this. They will need one when they turn 16.

7. National Insurance Number Requirements for Dependants

National Insurance Number requirements for dependants are specific. Here is what you need to know:

Requirement Details
Age 16 or over
Residency Living in the UK
Right to work Visa or immigration status must allow work
Identity documents Valid passport or BRP
Application Online through GOV.UK

What If You Do Not Meet the Requirements?

If you do not meet the requirements, you may not be able to apply for a National Insurance Number. Common reasons for ineligibility include:

  • Being under 16 years old
  • Not having the right to work in the UK
  • Not being resident in the UK
  • Not having acceptable identity documents

8. How to Apply for a National Insurance Number for Family Visa Holders

How to apply for a National Insurance Number for family visa holders follows the same process as for other applicants.

Step-by-Step Application Process

Step 1: Check Your Eligibility — Make sure you are eligible to apply. You must be 16 or over, living in the UK, and have the right to work.

Step 2: Check Your BRP — Before applying, check your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP). Your National Insurance Number may already be printed on the back.

Step 3: Go to the Official GOV.UK Website — Visit the official GOV.UK website to start your application. The application is completely free.

Step 4: Complete the Online Application — Fill in your personal details, including your name, date of birth, and address. You will also need to upload photos of your documents.

Step 5: Prove Your Identity — You will need to upload a photo of yourself holding your passport and upload photos of your identity documents.

Step 6: Submit Your Application — Once you have completed all sections, submit your application. You will receive a confirmation email.

Step 7: Wait for Processing — Your application will be processed by HMRC. This usually takes 4 to 8 weeks.

Step 8: Receive Your NI Number — You will receive your National Insurance Number by post within 4-8 weeks.

10. Dependants Right to Work UK and National Insurance Number

Dependants right to work UK is directly linked to your National Insurance Number.

What Is Right to Work?

Right to work is your legal permission to work in the UK. It is determined by your immigration status and visa conditions.

Do Dependants Have the Right to Work?

Most dependants and family visa holders have the right to work in the UK. However, you should always check your visa conditions:

Visa Type Right to Work
Spouse Visa (UK citizen) ✅ Full right to work
Spouse Visa (settled person) ✅ Full right to work
Parent Visa ✅ Full right to work
Child Visa (over 18) ✅ Full right to work (depending on visa)
Child Visa (under 18) ⚠️ Limited right to work
Dependants on other visas ✅ Depending on visa type

How to Prove Right to Work

You can prove your right to work by showing your passport or BRP, generating a share code on the GOV.UK website, or providing your visa documents.

11. National Insurance Number for Family Members UK

National Insurance Number for family members UK is available for various family members.

Who Can Apply

  • Spouses — Married or civil partners
  • Unmarried partners — Partners in a relationship akin to marriage
  • Children — Under 18 or over 18 with dependant status
  • Parents — Parents of UK residents or settled persons
  • Other relatives — In exceptional circumstances

Application Process

The application process is the same for all family members. You apply online through the official GOV.UK website.

Important Points

  • You must have the right to work in the UK
  • Your visa must allow work
  • You must be resident in the UK
  • The application is free

13. Biometric Residence Permit and National Insurance Number

Your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) and National Insurance Number are closely linked.

What Is a BRP?

A Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) is a document that proves your right to live, study, or work in the UK. It contains your personal details, visa information, and often your National Insurance Number.

Does Your BRP Have Your NI Number?

If you already have a National Insurance Number, it is often printed on the back of your BRP. Always check the back of your BRP before applying for a new NI Number.

What If Your BRP Does Not Have an NI Number?

If your BRP does not have an NI Number printed on it, you will need to apply for one through the official GOV.UK website.

Applying with a BRP

When you apply for a National Insurance Number, your BRP is one of the key documents you will need to provide. It proves your identity and your right to work in the UK.
