1. Why Students Need a National Insurance Number

If you're studying in the UK on a student visa and plan to take on part-time work, you'll need a National Insurance Number for international students to be paid correctly and to build up a record of your National Insurance contributions. It's separate from your student visa and university enrolment, but just as important for anyone planning to work alongside their studies.

You can start your part-time job before your number arrives — see our guide on whether you need a National Insurance Number to work — but you should apply as early as possible.

2. Eligibility for Student Visa Holders

Most students on a Student visa (formerly Tier 4) are eligible to apply, provided their visa conditions allow part-time work. This is generally capped at 20 hours per week during term time, with full-time work permitted during official holidays. Check the full National Insurance Number eligibility criteria if you're unsure whether your visa type qualifies.

4. Work Hour Rules and Your Number

Your National Insurance Number itself doesn't restrict how many hours you can work — that's governed by your visa conditions, not your NI record. However, having your number ready means your part-time employer can pay you accurately from your very first shift, rather than relying on a temporary tax code for weeks.

Student tip: Apply for your National Insurance Number as soon as you've enrolled and settled into UK accommodation, well before you start job hunting, so there's no delay once you're offered a role.

5. Timeline for Student Applications

Processing times for students are broadly similar to other applicants, generally 4 to 8 weeks, though this can vary depending on term-time demand at universities and whether an identity interview is needed. See our full NINO processing time guide for a detailed week-by-week breakdown.

6. Common Student Mistakes to Avoid

  • Waiting until a job offer arrives — apply as soon as you settle into UK accommodation
  • Using a temporary address — make sure your term-time address is accurate and stable
  • Forgetting your CAS number — this links your immigration record to your university
  • Assuming you can't work without a number — you can, as covered in our related guide above

Universities' international student support offices, along with resources from Citizens Advice, can also help if you run into any complications during your application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do international students need a National Insurance Number? +
Yes, if you plan to work part-time while studying, you'll need a National Insurance Number to be taxed correctly.
Can students work before receiving their National Insurance Number? +
Yes, you can start a part-time job before your number arrives, as long as you have the right to work under your visa.
What documents do international students need to apply? +
Typically a passport, student visa or BRP, CAS number, and proof of your UK term-time address.
How many hours can I work as a student with a National Insurance Number? +
Your National Insurance Number doesn't set your work hours; these are governed by your visa conditions, usually up to 20 hours per week in term time.
How long does it take for students to receive their number? +
Processing generally takes 4 to 8 weeks, similar to other applicant categories.
Can I apply before starting university? +
You generally need to be in the UK to apply, so most students apply shortly after arriving and settling in.
Does my National Insurance Number expire when I graduate? +
No, your National Insurance Number is permanent and stays with you regardless of your visa or study status changing.
What if I move accommodation during my studies? +
You should update your address with HMRC to make sure important letters, including your NI Number, reach you.
Can my university help with my application? +
Many universities' international student offices can offer general guidance, though the application itself is completed individually through HMRC.
