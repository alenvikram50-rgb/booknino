1. What Is a National Insurance Number?

A National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) is a unique personal reference number used by the UK tax system. It is made up of two letters, six numbers, and a final letter – for example, QQ 12 34 56 C.

This number stays with you for life and ensures that your National Insurance contributions are recorded correctly, the tax you pay is attributed to your name, and your entitlement to state benefits and the State Pension is tracked properly.

Important: Your National Insurance Number is not a form of identity. It is simply a reference number for the tax and benefits system managed by HMRC.

2. Who Needs a National Insurance Number for New Arrivals to the UK?

You need to apply for a National Insurance Number for new arrivals to the UK if you:

  • Live in the UK
  • Have the right to work in the UK
  • Are working, looking for work, or have an offer to start work

The law requires you to apply for an NI Number if you do not already have one and you are working or intend to work in the UK.

Do new arrivals need a National Insurance Number? Yes. If you have recently arrived in the UK and plan to work, you must apply for a National Insurance Number. Even if you are not working immediately but intend to look for work, it is wise to apply as soon as possible.

3. How to Apply for a National Insurance Number UK

The application process for a National Insurance Number for new arrivals is straightforward. Here is a step-by-step breakdown:

Step 1: Check If You Already Have One

Before applying, check if you already have a National Insurance Number. You might have one if:

  • You have a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) – the number may be printed on the back
  • You previously lived or worked in the UK
  • You received one automatically (UK citizens get one before their 16th birthday)

If you find your number on an old payslip, P60 form, or official HMRC correspondence, you do not need to apply again.

Step 2: Apply Online via GOV.UK

The application is entirely online through the official GOV.UK website. Here is what you need to do:

  1. Visit the GOV.UK application page – search "apply for a National Insurance Number" on GOV.UK
  2. Complete the online form – you will need to provide your full name, date of birth, UK address (including postcode), date of arrival in the UK, and current employment status
  3. Confirm your identity – you will need to provide details from your passport and confirm your immigration status
  4. Upload documents – you will be asked to upload a photo of your passport and a photo of yourself with your passport
  5. Provide contact details – leave a contact number in case there are questions about your application

Step 3: Receive Confirmation

After submitting your application, you will receive an email with an application reference number. The email will tell you if you need to provide further proof of identity.

Important Notes:
  • There is no fee – applying for a National Insurance Number is completely free
  • You must be in the UK – you can only apply when you are physically in the UK
  • No interview required – the process is now fully online
  • The application takes about 20 minutes to complete

5. Can I Work Before Getting a National Insurance Number?

This is one of the most common questions for new arrivals to the UK. The answer is yes – you can start work before your National Insurance Number arrives.

Here is what you need to know:

  • You can look for and start work without a National Insurance Number if you can prove you have the right to work in the UK
  • Your right to work can be proven with your BRP, visa, or a share code
  • Employers must conduct right-to-work checks, but having an NI Number is not part of these checks

What to Tell Your Employer

When you start work without an NI Number:

  1. Complete a Starter Checklist for your employer's payroll purposes
  2. Tell your employer that you have applied for a National Insurance Number
  3. Provide your NI Number as soon as you receive it

Your employer can begin processing your payroll even without your National Insurance Number – they simply leave that section blank, and HMRC will trace the number later.

Important: If you have a temporary National Insurance Number starting with 'TN', this is not a proper NI Number and will not be accepted by HMRC. You must apply for a permanent number.

If you need to check your application status, you can contact NI NO support for assistance.

7. What to Do After Receiving Your National Insurance Number

Once you receive your National Insurance Number letter, here are the next steps:

  1. Give it to your employer – your employer needs it for payroll and tax purposes
  2. Keep it safe – you will need this number for life for employment, tax purposes, benefits claims, and State Pension
  3. Access your Personal Tax Account – you can use your NI Number to set up a Personal Tax Account with HMRC to view your tax records and National Insurance contributions

If you lose your NI Number, you can apply for a replacement through our service.

8. National Insurance Contributions Explained

Your National Insurance Number is used to track your National Insurance contributions. These contributions are deducted from your earnings and go towards:

  • The State Pension
  • Certain state benefits
  • The NHS

The amount you contribute depends on your employment status and earnings. Keeping your contributions recorded correctly under your unique NI Number ensures you receive the benefits and pension you are entitled to.

For more information about how your NI contributions affect your State Pension, you can visit NI NO for guidance.

9. Special Cases: Students, EU Nationals, and More

Students

If you are an international student in the UK and plan to work (including part-time work during term time or holidays), you should apply for a National Insurance Number. You can start looking for work without one, but you will need it once you secure a job.

For specific guidance for students, visit our NI Number for Students page.

People Applying for Benefits

If you are applying for benefits, you do not need to apply separately for a National Insurance Number. The benefit team will start the process for you as part of your application.

EU Nationals

EU nationals moving to the UK should apply for a National Insurance Number as soon as they have a UK address. The process is the same as for other new arrivals.

13. Conclusion

Getting your National Insurance Number for new arrivals to the UK is an essential step for anyone planning to work in the UK. The process is straightforward, free, and can be completed online.
