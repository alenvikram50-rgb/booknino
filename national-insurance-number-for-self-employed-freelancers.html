1. National Insurance Number for Self-Employed & Freelancers

If you are planning to work for yourself in the United Kingdom, understanding the National Insurance Number for Self-Employed & Freelancers is absolutely essential. Whether you are a freelancer, a sole trader, a contractor, or running your own small business, you need to know how the UK tax and National Insurance system works.

A National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) is a unique reference number used by HMRC (HM Revenue & Customs) to identify you within the UK's tax and benefits system. For self-employed individuals and freelancers, having a National Insurance Number is not optional — it is a legal requirement for paying the correct taxes and National Insurance contributions.

The National Insurance Number for self-employed individuals serves the same basic purpose as it does for employees: it tracks your National Insurance contributions throughout your working life. However, the way you pay these contributions is different for self-employed people.

If you are a freelancer or self-employed, you will need to pay two types of National Insurance contributions: Class 2 National Insurance (a flat weekly rate) and Class 4 National Insurance (a percentage of your annual profits). Your National Insurance Number ensures that all your contributions are correctly recorded against your name, helping you build your State Pension entitlement and access other state benefits when needed.

In this comprehensive guide, we will explain everything you need to know about the National Insurance Number for Self-Employed & Freelancers, including how to get one, how to register as self-employed with HMRC, and how to pay your National Insurance contributions.

2. What is a National Insurance Number for Self-Employed?

What is a National Insurance Number for self-employed individuals? It is the same unique reference number that everyone working in the UK must have. Your National Insurance Number identifies you to HMRC and ensures that all your tax and National Insurance contributions are correctly recorded.

For self-employed people, your National Insurance Number is used for:

  • Registering as self-employed — You need your NI Number to register with HMRC
  • Paying Class 2 National Insurance — Your NI Number tracks your weekly flat-rate contributions
  • Paying Class 4 National Insurance — Your NI Number tracks your profit-based contributions
  • Filing Self Assessment tax returns — Your NI Number identifies you to HMRC
  • Building your State Pension — Your contributions are recorded against your NI Number
  • Claiming benefits — Your NI Number determines your benefit entitlement

If you are self-employed, you must have a National Insurance Number. Without one, you cannot register as self-employed with HMRC, you cannot file your Self Assessment tax return, and you cannot build your State Pension entitlement.

3. Do Freelancers Need a National Insurance Number in the UK?

Do freelancers need a National Insurance Number in the UK? The short answer is yes. If you are a freelancer working in the UK, you need a National Insurance Number to work legally and pay the correct taxes.

Why Freelancers Need an NI Number

Reason Explanation
Legal Requirement You must have an NI Number to work in the UK
Register with HMRC You need it to register as self-employed
Pay National Insurance Your contributions are tracked with your NI Number
File Tax Returns Your NI Number is required for Self Assessment
State Pension Your contributions build your State Pension
Claim Benefits You need an NI Number to claim benefits

What Happens If You Don't Have One?

If you are a freelancer and you do not have a National Insurance Number, you cannot: legally work in the UK, register as self-employed with HMRC, pay the correct National Insurance contributions, file your Self Assessment tax return, build your State Pension entitlement, or claim state benefits.

Important: If you are a freelancer or self-employed and you do not have a National Insurance Number, you must apply for one immediately. You cannot legally operate as a self-employed person in the UK without one.

4. How to Get a National Insurance Number as a Self-Employed Person

How to get a National Insurance Number as a self-employed person is a common question for those starting their own business or freelance career. Here is the step-by-step process:

Step 1: Check If You Already Have One

Before applying, check if you already have a National Insurance Number. You may have been assigned one when you turned 16 if you are a UK citizen. If you have worked in the UK before, you likely already have one.

Step 2: Check Your Documents

If you do not have an NI Number, check your documents: UK citizens should have received a letter shortly before their 16th birthday. Non-UK citizens may have their NI Number printed on the back of their biometric residence permit (BRP).

Step 3: Apply Online

If you do not have an NI Number, you can apply online through the official GOV.UK website.

Step 4: Prove Your Identity

You will need to provide your passport, biometric residence permit (BRP) if you have one, proof of your right to work in the UK, and your UK address.

Step 5: Wait for Processing

Your application will be processed by HMRC. This usually takes 4 to 8 weeks. Check our processing times guide for more details.

Step 6: Receive Your NI Number

You will receive your National Insurance Number by post to your UK address.

Pro tip: You can register as self-employed with HMRC before you receive your National Insurance Number. However, you must provide your NI Number to HMRC as soon as you receive it. For help with your application, apply through NI NO and we will handle everything for you.

5. How to Apply for a National Insurance Number for Self-Employment

How to apply for a National Insurance Number for self-employment follows the same process as applying for any other reason. Here is what you need to know:

Who Can Apply

You can apply for a National Insurance Number if you are aged 16 or over, live in the UK, have the right to work in the UK, and plan to work (including self-employment).

Documents You Need

Document Purpose
Passport Proof of identity and nationality
Biometric Residence Permit Proof of immigration status
Visa Documents Proof of right to work
Proof of UK Address To receive your NI Number letter

The Application Process

  1. Visit GOV.UK — Go to the official application page
  2. Create a Government Gateway account — You will need this to apply
  3. Complete the application form — Provide your personal details
  4. Upload your documents — Take and upload photos of your documents
  5. Take a photo — You will need a photo of yourself holding your passport
  6. Submit your application — Review and submit
  7. Wait for processing — This takes 4-8 weeks
  8. Receive your NI Number — It will arrive by post

What If You Need to Start Self-Employment Immediately?

You can start your self-employment while your National Insurance Number application is being processed. However, you must: apply immediately, register as self-employed with HMRC (you can do this without your NI Number), provide your NI Number to HMRC as soon as you receive it, and keep records of your income and expenses.

6. Can I Be Self-Employed Without a National Insurance Number?

Can I be self-employed without a National Insurance Number? The answer is no, not legally.

Why You Cannot Be Self-Employed Without an NI Number

  • Legal requirement — You must have an NI Number to work in the UK
  • HMRC registration — You cannot register as self-employed without one
  • Tax compliance — You cannot pay the correct taxes without one
  • National Insurance — You cannot pay Class 2 and Class 4 contributions without one

What You Can Do

While you cannot legally be self-employed without a National Insurance Number, you can: start your self-employment activities, register as self-employed with HMRC (using your name and address), apply for your National Insurance Number immediately, and provide your NI Number to HMRC as soon as you receive it.

Important: You must apply for your National Insurance Number immediately if you are self-employed. You cannot operate as a self-employed person in the UK without one. The application is free and straightforward.

8. Self-Employed National Insurance UK

Self-Employed National Insurance UK — understanding your contributions is essential for managing your tax affairs. Here is what you need to know:

Two Types of Contributions

As a self-employed person in the UK, you pay two types of National Insurance contributions:

Type Rate Who Pays
Class 2 Flat weekly rate (£3.45 per week in 2025/26) All self-employed people with profits above the small profits threshold
Class 4 Percentage of annual profits (6% and 2% in 2025/26) Self-employed people with profits above the threshold

Small Profits Threshold

If your annual profits are below the small profits threshold (currently £6,725), you do not need to pay Class 2 contributions. However, you can still choose to pay them voluntarily to protect your State Pension entitlement.

How Contributions Are Recorded

Your National Insurance Number is used to track all your contributions. Every Class 2 and Class 4 payment you make is recorded against your NI Number, building your contribution record for the State Pension and other benefits.

9. Freelance National Insurance UK

Freelance National Insurance UK — if you are a freelancer, here is what you need to know about National Insurance:

You Are Responsible for Your Own Contributions

As a freelancer, you are responsible for paying your own National Insurance contributions. Unlike employees, where the employer deducts National Insurance from your pay, freelancers must pay their contributions directly to HMRC.

How to Pay Your Contributions

Method Details
Self Assessment You pay Class 4 contributions through your Self Assessment tax return
Direct Debit You can set up a direct debit to pay Class 2 contributions
Bank Transfer You can make payments directly to HMRC
Annual Payment You can pay your Class 4 contributions as part of your tax bill

When to Pay

  • Class 2 — Usually paid monthly or annually (optional if profits are below the small profits threshold)
  • Class 4 — Paid through your Self Assessment tax return (usually by 31 January)
Freelancer tip: Set aside money for your National Insurance contributions regularly. It is a good idea to put aside a percentage of your income each month to cover your Class 2 and Class 4 contributions.

10. National Insurance Contributions for Self-Employed Workers

National Insurance contributions for self-employed workers are different from those for employees. Here is a detailed breakdown:

Class 2 Contributions

  • Rate — Flat weekly rate (£3.45 per week in 2025/26)
  • Who pays — Self-employed people with profits above the small profits threshold
  • How to pay — Monthly or annually through direct debit or bank transfer
  • Purpose — Builds your State Pension entitlement

Class 4 Contributions

  • Rate — Percentage of your annual profits (6% on profits between £12,570 and £50,270, 2% on profits above £50,270)
  • Who pays — Self-employed people with profits above the Class 4 threshold
  • How to pay — Through your Self Assessment tax return
  • Purpose — Builds your State Pension and benefit entitlement

How Contributions Are Calculated

Profits Class 2 Class 4
Below £6,725 Not required Not required
£6,725 to £12,570 Optional voluntary Not required
£12,570 to £50,270 Required 6% of profits
Above £50,270 Required 6% on first £50,270, 2% on remaining

Your Contribution Record

All your contributions are recorded against your National Insurance Number. This record determines your eligibility for the State Pension and other benefits.

11. Class 2 National Insurance and Class 4 National Insurance Explained

Understanding Class 2 National Insurance and Class 4 National Insurance is essential for self-employed individuals.

Class 2 National Insurance

Class 2 National Insurance is a flat weekly rate paid by self-employed people. It is designed to build your State Pension entitlement.

Feature Details
Type Flat weekly rate
Rate (2025/26) £3.45 per week
Payable If profits above £6,725
Payment Monthly or annually
Purpose State Pension entitlement

Class 4 National Insurance

Class 4 National Insurance is a percentage of your annual profits. It is paid through your Self Assessment tax return.

Feature Details
Type Percentage of profits
Rate (2025/26) 6% (£12,570 - £50,270), 2% (above £50,270)
Payable If profits above £12,570
Payment Through Self Assessment
Purpose State Pension and benefits

Your National Insurance Number

Your National Insurance Number tracks both Class 2 and Class 4 contributions. Without an NI Number, your contributions cannot be recorded correctly.

12. Register as Self-Employed with HMRC

Register as self-employed with HMRC is an essential step for anyone starting a freelance business.

When to Register

You must register as self-employed with HMRC by 5 October of your business's second tax year, or as soon as possible if you are starting your business.

How to Register

  1. Go to GOV.UK — Visit the official registration page
  2. Create a Government Gateway account — If you do not have one
  3. Complete the registration form — Provide your details
  4. Wait for confirmation — You will receive your UTR Number (Unique Taxpayer Reference)

Documents You Need

  • Your National Insurance Number
  • Your personal details
  • Your business details
  • Your UK address

What If You Don't Have an NI Number?

If you do not have a National Insurance Number, you can still register as self-employed. However, you must: apply for your NI Number immediately, provide your NI Number to HMRC as soon as you receive it, and you cannot file your first Self Assessment without an NI Number.

Important: You must register as self-employed with HMRC by 5 October of your business's second tax year. Failure to register on time can result in penalties. Contact NI NO if you need help with registration.

13. National Insurance and Self Assessment

National Insurance and Self Assessment are closely linked for self-employed individuals.

What Is Self Assessment?

Self Assessment is the system used by HMRC to collect Income Tax and National Insurance from self-employed individuals. You must file a Self Assessment tax return each year if you are self-employed.

How Your NI Contributions Are Paid Through Self Assessment

Your Class 4 National Insurance contributions are calculated on your Self Assessment tax return and included in your tax bill. Your Class 2 contributions are also linked to your Self Assessment record.

Filing Your Tax Return

When you file your Self Assessment tax return, you will: report your income and expenses, calculate your profits, calculate your Class 4 National Insurance contributions, calculate your Income Tax, and pay what you owe by the deadlines.

Deadlines

Deadline What For
5 October Register as self-employed
31 January File online tax return
31 January Pay tax and Class 4 contributions
31 July Payment on account (if applicable)

How to pay National Insurance as a freelancer is a common question. Here is what you need to know:

Class 2 National Insurance Payments

Method Details
Direct Debit Set up a monthly direct debit
Bank Transfer Pay directly to HMRC
Annual Payment Pay the full year in advance
Voluntary Payments Pay even if not required

Class 4 National Insurance Payments

Method Details
Through Self Assessment Included in your tax bill
Bank Transfer Pay directly to HMRC
Payment on Account Advance payments (if applicable)

Making Payments

  1. Calculate what you owe — Use your Self Assessment tax return or your records
  2. Choose a payment method — Direct debit, bank transfer, or cheque
  3. Pay by the deadline — 31 January for the tax year
  4. Keep records — Keep proof of your payments

What Happens If You Don't Pay?

  • Penalties — You will be charged penalties and interest
  • Contribution record — Your State Pension record may be affected
  • Legal action — HMRC can take legal action
Freelancer tip: Set up a direct debit to pay your Class 2 contributions automatically. This ensures your contributions are always up to date and your State Pension record is protected.

15. First-Time Self-Employed National Insurance Guide

First-time self-employed National Insurance guide — if you are starting your first freelance business, here is everything you need to know:

Step 1: Get Your National Insurance Number

If you do not already have a National Insurance Number, apply for one immediately. You cannot operate as a self-employed person without one.

Step 2: Register as Self-Employed with HMRC

Register as self-employed on the GOV.UK website. You will need your National Insurance Number to complete the registration.

Step 3: Understand Your National Insurance Contributions

Learn about Class 2 and Class 4 National Insurance contributions. Know what you need to pay and how to pay it.

Step 4: Keep Records

Keep records of your income and expenses. You will need these for your Self Assessment tax return.

Step 5: File Your Self Assessment

File your Self Assessment tax return each year. This is how you report your income, calculate your tax, and pay your Class 4 National Insurance contributions.

Step 6: Pay Your Contributions

Pay your Class 2 contributions monthly or annually. Pay your Class 4 contributions through your Self Assessment tax return.

Step 7: Plan for the Future

Use your Personal Tax Account to check your National Insurance record. Make sure your contributions are being recorded correctly.
