1. What is a National Insurance Number?

A National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) is a unique reference number used by HMRC (HM Revenue & Customs) to identify you within the UK's tax and benefits system. It ensures that the National Insurance contributions and tax you pay are properly recorded against your name.

Think of it as your personal tax and contributions identifier that follows you throughout your working life in the United Kingdom. Every time you are paid, your employer uses your NI Number to deduct the correct amount of tax and National Insurance through the PAYE (Pay As You Earn) system.

Your National Insurance Number is also used as a reference when communicating with the Government Department for Work and Pensions and HMRC. It is essential for:

  • Working legally in the UK
  • Paying the correct tax and National Insurance
  • Building your State Pension entitlement
  • Accessing certain state benefits (if eligible)

For Skilled Worker Visa holders, understanding the importance of the National Insurance Number is essential from the moment you arrive in the UK.

2. Do Skilled Worker Visa Holders Need a National Insurance Number?

Do Skilled Worker Visa holders need a National Insurance Number? The short answer is yes.

If you are coming to the UK on a Skilled Worker Visa, you will need a National Insurance Number to work legally in the UK. This is because your NI Number is required for your UK employment income to be correctly taxed.

Why Skilled Worker Visa Holders Need an NI Number

There are several reasons why a National Insurance Number for Skilled Worker Visa holders is essential:

  • Legal Requirement – By law, you must have a permanent National Insurance Number once you start working in the UK. Your employer will need it to process your payroll correctly.
  • Correct Tax Deductions – Your employer uses your NI Number to deduct the correct amount of income tax and National Insurance from your salary through the PAYE system.
  • National Insurance Contributions – Your NI Number tracks your National Insurance contributions, which are important for your State Pension and other benefits.
  • Employment Record – Your National Insurance Number links all your employment records together, ensuring that your tax and contributions are correctly recorded.

Important: Your NI Number May Already Be on Your BRP

If you have applied for a Skilled Worker Visa, the Home Office should, by default, issue you with a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) that states your UK National Insurance Number. This is because, as a Skilled Worker, you will be coming to the UK to take up full-time employment.

In such cases, your NINO will appear on your eVisa profile or BRP, and there is no need for you to make a separate application to obtain one.

Key Tip: Always check your BRP first – you may already have your National Insurance Number without needing to apply!

3. Can I Work Without a National Insurance Number in the UK?

Can I work without a National Insurance Number in the UK? This is one of the most common questions for Skilled Worker Visa holders.

Short Answer: Yes, But...

Yes, you can start work without a National Insurance Number as long as you can prove you have the right to work in the UK. However, there are important conditions:

  • You must apply for your NI Number immediately
  • You must provide your NI Number to your employer as soon as you receive it
  • Your employer can begin payroll even without your National Insurance Number – you just need to provide it once you receive it

What You Need to Do

  1. Start your job – You can begin working as soon as you have the right to work
  2. Apply for your NI Number – Do this as soon as possible after arriving in the UK
  3. Inform your employer – Let them know you have applied and will provide your number when it arrives
  4. Give your employer your NI Number – As soon as you receive it, provide it to your employer
Important: Your National Insurance Number is not proof of your right to work – your visa or share code is. You can only apply for an NI Number once you have arrived in the UK.

4. How to Apply for a National Insurance Number on a Skilled Worker Visa

How to apply for a National Insurance Number on a Skilled Worker Visa is a straightforward process. Here is a step-by-step guide:

Step 1: Check If You Already Have an NI Number

Before applying, check your Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) or eVisa profile. If you have applied for a Skilled Worker Visa, the Home Office may have already issued you a National Insurance Number.

If your NI Number is on your BRP or eVisa, you do not need to apply – you already have one!

Step 2: Start Your Application Online

If you do not already have an NI Number, you need to apply online through the official GOV.UK website: gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number

Step 3: Create or Sign In to Government Gateway

You will need a Government Gateway user ID and password. If you do not have one, you can create one during the application process.

Step 4: Verify Your Identity

You will be asked to verify your identity – either via the GOV.UK One Login service or by submitting documents.

Step 5: Complete the Application Form

Fill in the online form with your personal details, including your full name, date of birth, current UK address, immigration status, and right to work details.

Step 6: Submit and Wait

After submission, you will receive a confirmation. Your application will be processed, and you will receive your National Insurance Number by post.

Remember: You can only apply for an NI Number once you have arrived in the UK. You cannot apply from overseas.

8. How to Get an NI Number After Arriving in the UK

How to get an NI Number after arriving in the UK is a priority for many Skilled Worker Visa holders.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Arrive in the UK – Make sure you have entered the UK with your valid Skilled Worker Visa.
  2. Check Your BRP – Look at your Biometric Residence Permit – your National Insurance Number may already be printed on it.
  3. If You Don't Have One, Apply Online – Go to gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number and complete the online application.
  4. Provide Your Documents – Upload clear photos of your identity documents.
  5. Attend an Interview (If Required) – You may be asked to attend an Evidence of Identity interview at a Jobcentre Plus office.
  6. Receive Your NI Number – Your National Insurance Number will be posted to your UK address within 4-8 weeks.

Important Tips

  • Apply as soon as possible after arriving in the UK
  • Check your BRP first – you may already have your NI Number
  • You can start work while your application is being processed
  • Keep your NI Number safe – it is yours for life
