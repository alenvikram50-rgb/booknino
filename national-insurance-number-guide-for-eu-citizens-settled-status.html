1. National Insurance Number Guide for EU Citizens (Settled Status)

If you are an EU citizen living in the UK with settled status, you may be wondering about your National Insurance Number. This National Insurance Number guide for EU citizens (settled status) will explain everything you need to know.

A National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) is a unique reference number used by HMRC (HM Revenue & Customs) to identify you within the UK's tax and benefits system. It is essential for working, paying tax, claiming benefits, and building your State Pension entitlement.

Since the UK left the European Union, the rules for EU citizens have changed. However, if you have settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme, you have the right to live and work in the UK indefinitely. This means you are eligible to apply for a National Insurance Number if you do not already have one.

This comprehensive guide is designed specifically for EU citizens with settled status. We will cover everything from eligibility and application to documents and processing times. Whether you are looking for work, already employed, or planning for your future in the UK, this National Insurance Number guide for EU citizens (settled status) will help you understand what you need to do.

2. What is Settled Status and How Does It Relate to Your NI Number?

Settled status is a UK immigration status granted to EU, EEA, or Swiss citizens who were living in the UK before the end of the transition period on 31 December 2020. It was introduced under the EU Settlement Scheme to protect the rights of EU citizens after Brexit.

What Settled Status Gives You

  • The right to live and work in the UK indefinitely
  • Access to public services like the NHS
  • Eligibility for benefits and pensions
  • The right to apply for British citizenship (after meeting certain requirements)

How It Relates to Your National Insurance Number

Your settled status does not automatically give you a National Insurance Number. However, it does give you the right to work in the UK, which means you are eligible to apply for one.

If you already have a National Insurance Number, you do not need a new one. Your number stays with you for life, even if your immigration status changes.

If you do not have a National Insurance Number, you must apply for one if you plan to work in the UK. Having settled status makes you eligible to apply.

Pre-Settled Status vs Settled Status

Pre-settled status is a temporary status given to EU citizens who had not yet lived in the UK for five continuous years when they applied. After five years of continuous residence, pre-settled status holders can apply for settled status.

Both pre-settled status and settled status holders are eligible to apply for a National Insurance Number. The Home Office may also use your National Insurance records to automatically convert some pre-settled status holders to settled status.

3. Do EU Citizens with Settled Status Need a National Insurance Number?

Do EU citizens with settled status need a National Insurance Number? The short answer is: yes, if you plan to work in the UK.

Why You Need a National Insurance Number

  • To work legally — Your employer needs your National Insurance Number to process your payroll, deduct the correct tax and National Insurance, and report your earnings to HMRC.
  • To pay the correct tax — Without an NI Number, you may be placed on an emergency tax code and pay more tax than you should.
  • To claim benefits — If you need to claim certain state benefits, you will need a National Insurance Number.
  • To build your State Pension — Your National Insurance contributions are recorded against your number and determine your State Pension entitlement.
  • To access your Personal Tax Account — Your NI Number gives you access to your online tax records.

What If You Do Not Plan to Work?

If you have settled status but do not plan to work in the UK, you do not necessarily need a National Insurance Number. However, it is still a good idea to apply for one in case your circumstances change. You can apply even if you do not have a job yet.

Important Note

You do not need a National Insurance Number to apply for settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme. However, providing your NI Number can help the Home Office verify your residence in the UK more easily.

4. Can You Work Without a National Insurance Number If You Have Settled Status?

Can you work without a National Insurance Number if you have settled status? Yes, you can start work without a National Insurance Number as long as you can prove your right to work in the UK.

How to Prove Your Right to Work

As an EU citizen with settled status, you can prove your right to work by:

  • Using your settled status confirmation (digital or paper)
  • Generating a share code online through the UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) service
  • Showing your biometric residence permit (BRP) if you have one

What Happens If You Start Work Without an NI Number?

  • You can start work immediately
  • Your employer will use a temporary reference number
  • You will need to apply for your National Insurance Number as soon as possible
  • Once you receive your number, you must give it to your employer
  • You may be placed on an emergency tax code until your number is provided

Important Reminder

While you can start work without a National Insurance Number, you must apply for one immediately. You cannot work legally in the UK without one in the long term.

The National Insurance Number requirements for EU citizens are straightforward. Here is what you need:

Basic Requirements

Requirement Details
Age You must be aged 16 or over
Residency You must live in the UK
Right to Work You must have the right to work in the UK
Employment Status You must be working, looking for work, or have an offer to start work

EU Citizens with Settled Status

If you have settled status under the EU Settlement Scheme, you meet the right to work requirement. This means you are fully eligible to apply for a National Insurance Number.

EU Citizens with Pre-Settled Status

If you have pre-settled status, you are also eligible to apply. Pre-settled status gives you the right to work in the UK, which is the key requirement for a National Insurance Number.

What If You Are Not Working Yet?

You do not need to have a job to apply. You can apply for a National Insurance Number if you are looking for work or have an offer to start work.

6. How to Apply for a National Insurance Number with Settled Status

How to apply for a National Insurance Number with settled status is a simple process. Here is the step-by-step guide:

Step 1: Check If You Already Have a National Insurance Number

Before applying, check if you already have a National Insurance Number. You may have one if:

  • You previously worked in the UK
  • You had a National Insurance Number in the past
  • Your biometric residence permit (BRP) has a number on the back

You can also check your Personal Tax Account or the HMRC app to see if you already have a number.

Step 2: Apply Online

You can apply online through the official GOV.UK website at gov.uk/apply-national-insurance-number.

Step 3: Prove Your Identity

You will need to prove your identity using acceptable documents. For EU citizens, this usually means:

  • Your passport (from any country)
  • Your national identity card from an EU country, Norway, Liechtenstein, or Switzerland

If you have a biometric residence permit (BRP) or an eVisa, you may already have a National Insurance Number.

Step 4: Complete the Application Form

You will need to provide:

  • Your personal details (name, date of birth, address)
  • Your immigration status (including your settled status details)
  • Information about your right to work in the UK

Step 5: Submit and Wait

Once you submit your application, it will be reviewed by HMRC. You may be asked to attend an identity interview at a Jobcentre Plus office if your identity cannot be verified digitally.

Step 6: Receive Your National Insurance Number

You will receive your National Insurance Number by post. It can take up to 4 weeks to arrive after your identity has been verified.

7. Documents Needed for a National Insurance Number Application

The documents needed for a National Insurance Number application for EU citizens with settled status include:

Acceptable Identity Documents

Document Type Details
Passport A valid passport from any country
National Identity Card From an EU country, Norway, Liechtenstein, or Switzerland
Biometric Residence Permit (BRP) If you have one, check the back for your NI Number
eVisa Your UKVI account may show your NI Number

Additional Documents That May Be Required

  • Proof of your right to work — Your settled or pre-settled status confirmation
  • Proof of address — A utility bill, bank statement, or tenancy agreement
  • Proof of employment or job offer — A contract or offer letter (if available)

Important Notes

  • You can apply even if you do not have all the documents listed above
  • Documents must be original and valid
  • You may need to attend an identity interview if your documents cannot be verified digitally

If you have lost your documents, you can request a replacement NI Number letter through NI NO.

8. How Long Does It Take to Get a National Insurance Number in the UK?

How long does it take to get a National Insurance Number in the UK? The answer depends on your circumstances.

Standard Processing Time

It can take up to 4 weeks to receive your National Insurance Number once your identity has been verified.

Factors That Can Affect Processing Time

Factor Impact
Digital Identity Verification Faster — 2 to 4 weeks
In-Person Identity Interview Slower — 6 to 10 weeks
Document Quality Poor quality photos can cause delays
Application Volume Peak periods (September, January) may slow processing
Postal Delivery Allow 3-5 working days for delivery

What If You Have Not Received Your Number?

If you have not received your National Insurance Number after 4 weeks:

  • Check that you provided the correct address
  • Look for any communication from HMRC
  • Contact HMRC if it has been more than 4 weeks

You will need your application reference number, which you can find in the confirmation email you received after applying.

9. National Insurance Number for Pre-Settled Status Holders

The National Insurance Number for pre-settled status holders is an important topic for EU citizens who have not yet reached five years of continuous residence in the UK.

Are Pre-Settled Status Holders Eligible?

Yes, EU citizens with pre-settled status are eligible to apply for a National Insurance Number. Pre-settled status gives you the right to work in the UK, which is the key requirement for a National Insurance Number.

Why You Should Apply

  • You can work legally in the UK
  • You can pay the correct tax and National Insurance
  • You can build your State Pension record
  • Your National Insurance records can help you prove your residence when applying for settled status

Automatic Conversion to Settled Status

The Home Office plans to automatically convert some eligible pre-settled status holders to settled status. This involves checking your UK residency records using your National Insurance number. Having a National Insurance Number can make this process smoother.

10. National Insurance Number and the EU Settlement Scheme

The National Insurance Number and the EU Settlement Scheme are closely connected. Here is what you need to know:

You Do Not Need an NI Number to Apply for Settled Status

You do not need a National Insurance Number to apply to the EU Settlement Scheme. You can apply without one.

Providing Your NI Number Can Help

If you provide your National Insurance Number when applying for settled status, the Home Office can:

  • Carry out automated checks of your UK tax and benefits records
  • Verify your residence in the UK more easily
  • Potentially grant settled status without requiring additional documents

If You Do Not Have an NI Number

If you do not have a National Insurance Number, your application to the EU Settlement Scheme will not be refused solely because you did not provide one. You can provide other evidence of residence instead.

11. What to Do If You Already Have a National Insurance Number

If you already have a National Insurance Number, you do not need to apply for a new one.

Your Number Stays With You for Life

Your National Insurance Number remains the same for life, even if:

  • Your personal details change
  • Your immigration status changes
  • You leave the UK and return later

How to Find Your Existing Number

You can find your National Insurance Number on:

  • Your payslip or P60
  • Letters from HMRC about benefits or tax
  • Your Personal Tax Account
  • The HMRC app
  • The back of your biometric residence permit (BRP) if you have one

If You Cannot Find Your Number

If you cannot find your National Insurance Number, you can:

  • Use the online service to find it
  • Contact HMRC to request a letter confirming your number
  • Complete form CA5403 to get your number by post

You can also request a replacement NI Number letter through NI NO.

12. Benefits of Having a National Insurance Number for EU Citizens

Having a National Insurance Number provides many benefits for EU citizens living in the UK:

Employment Benefits

  • Legal right to work — You cannot work legally in the UK without one
  • Correct tax deductions — Your employer can deduct the right amount of tax and National Insurance
  • Employment record — Your NI Number links your employment history across different jobs

Financial Benefits

  • State Pension — Your contributions are recorded against your number
  • Benefit entitlement — You can claim certain state benefits
  • Tax refunds — HMRC can process tax refunds correctly

Social Security Benefits

  • Access to your Personal Tax Account — View your tax and National Insurance records online
  • Student loans — Your NI Number is used to track student loan repayments
  • Universal Credit — Required for claiming Universal Credit

Long-Term Benefits

  • Building your State Pension — Your contributions determine your pension entitlement
  • Proving your residence — Your NI Number can help prove your residence in the UK
  • Automatic conversion — Helps the Home Office convert pre-settled status to settled status
