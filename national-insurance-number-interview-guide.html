1. Why You've Been Invited to an Interview

Not every applicant needs a National Insurance Number interview. HMRC first attempts to verify your identity digitally, and only invites you to attend in person or by video call when this isn't possible — for example, if your documents can't be automatically matched against Home Office or passport records.

If you're at this stage, your application is still progressing, just through a manual verification route. It's worth revisiting the documents required for your NINO application to make sure everything matches what you originally submitted.

2. What Happens at the Interview

The interview itself is generally short and straightforward. In most cases it's held at a Jobcentre Plus, though some appointments are now conducted remotely by phone or video call.

  • You'll be asked to confirm your personal details
  • An officer will check your original identity documents in person or on screen
  • You may be asked simple questions about your circumstances, such as your address or employer
  • The appointment usually lasts between 15 and 30 minutes

This is a routine identity-verification step, not an assessment of your immigration status, so there's no need to feel anxious about attending.

4. Interview Preparation Checklist

Arrive Early

Aim to arrive 10-15 minutes before your appointment time.

Bring Originals

Copies or photos are not usually accepted at in-person interviews.

Check Your Device

For video interviews, test your camera and internet connection beforehand.

Answer Clearly

Give straightforward, honest answers about your circumstances.

5. After Your Interview

Once your identity has been confirmed, your application moves back into standard processing. You can expect your number to be issued within a further one to two weeks, in line with the timelines covered in our NINO processing time guide.

If you don't hear back within a reasonable period after your interview, it's worth checking in with NI NO or contacting HMRC directly for an update.

6. Common Interview Questions

  • Can you confirm your full name and date of birth?
  • What is your current UK address?
  • Are you currently employed, and if so, by whom?
  • How long do you intend to stay in the UK?
  • Can you confirm the details on your visa or BRP?

For general guidance on attending official appointments and your rights during identity checks, GOV.UK's visas and immigration section is a helpful reference.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why have I been asked to attend a National Insurance Number interview? +
You're usually invited to an interview when HMRC cannot verify your identity digitally using the documents you submitted online.
What should I bring to my NI Number interview? +
Bring your original passport or BRP, proof of address, any visa documents, and your application reference number.
How long does the interview take? +
Most identity-check interviews last between 15 and 30 minutes.
Is the interview held in person or online? +
It depends on your circumstances; some interviews are held at a Jobcentre Plus in person, while others are conducted by phone or video call.
What questions will I be asked? +
Expect straightforward questions confirming your identity, address, and immigration status, rather than an assessment of your case.
What happens after the interview? +
Your application returns to standard processing, and your National Insurance Number is usually issued within one to two weeks.
Can I reschedule my interview? +
In most cases yes, though you should contact HMRC as soon as possible if you need to change your appointment.
Do I need to bring photocopies as well as originals? +
Originals are usually sufficient and preferred; photocopies are not typically required for in-person appointments.
What if I miss my interview? +
Missing your appointment without rescheduling can significantly delay your application, so contact HMRC promptly if this happens.
