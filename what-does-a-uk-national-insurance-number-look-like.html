1. What Does a UK National Insurance Number Look Like?

If you have ever wondered what does a UK National Insurance Number look like, you are not alone. The National Insurance Number (often called an NI Number or NINO) has a very specific format that is consistent for every person in the United Kingdom.

A UK National Insurance Number is made up of a combination of letters and numbers that follow a strict pattern. It is not random — there are specific rules about which letters can be used and where they can appear.

The standard format of a National Insurance Number is:

Two letters, followed by six numbers, followed by a single letter.

When written in full, it looks like this:

AB 12 34 56 C

This is the most common way you will see a National Insurance Number example — with spaces between each group to make it easier to read. However, it can also be written without spaces as AB123456C.

So, what does a National Insurance Number look like? It looks like a combination of letters and numbers that follows this exact pattern. In this comprehensive guide, we will explain everything you need to know about the UK National Insurance Number format.

3. UK National Insurance Number Format Explained

The UK National Insurance Number format follows a very specific structure that has been in place for decades. Here is a detailed breakdown:

The First Two Letters

The first two characters of your National Insurance Number are always letters. However, not all letters can be used:

  • The first letter cannot be D, F, I, Q, U, or V
  • The second letter cannot be D, F, I, O, Q, U, or V

This means that certain combinations like AA, AB, AC, AE, and others are valid, while combinations like DA, DF, II, or QU are not valid.

Some common starting letter combinations include: AA, AB, AC, AE, AG, AH, AJ, AK, AL, AM, AN, AP, AR, AS, AT, AW, AX, AY, BA, BB, BC, BE, BG, BH, BJ, BK, BL, BM, BN, BP, BR, BS, BT, BW, BX, BY, CA, CB, CC, CE, CG, CH, CJ, CK, CL, CM, CN, CP, CR, CS, CT, CW, CX, CY.

The Six Numbers

The middle part of your National Insurance Number consists of six numbers between 0 and 9. These numbers can be any combination, from 000000 to 999999.

In written format, these six numbers are often split into two groups of three with a space in between: AB 12 34 56 C or AB 123 456 C. Both formats are acceptable, but the most common way to write it is with a space after the first two letters and then the numbers in two groups of three.

The Final Letter

The last character of your National Insurance Number is always a letter, and it must be one of four letters: A, B, C, or D. This final letter is sometimes called the "suffix" and was historically used to indicate the sequence in which National Insurance Numbers were issued.

So, a complete National Insurance Number example would be: AB 12 34 56 C

5. National Insurance Number Format UK

The National Insurance Number format UK is strictly regulated by HMRC. Here are the official rules:

Valid Characters

  • The first character must be a letter (not D, F, I, Q, U, or V)
  • The second character must be a letter (not D, F, I, O, Q, U, or V)
  • The next six characters must be numbers (0-9)
  • The final character must be a letter (A, B, C, or D)

Invalid Examples

  • DA 12 34 56 C (invalid — D cannot be the first letter)
  • AB 12 34 56 F (invalid — F cannot be the final letter)
  • AB 12 34 56 (invalid — missing the final letter)
  • AB 12 34 56 7 C (invalid — too many numbers)

Valid Examples

  • AA 12 34 56 C (valid)
  • AB 12 34 56 C (valid)
  • AC 12 34 56 C (valid)
  • AE 12 34 56 C (valid)

6. How is a National Insurance Number Written?

How is a National Insurance Number written? There are several ways you might see your NI Number written:

  • With Spaces (Most Common): AB 12 34 56 C — This is the most common format. The spaces make the number easier to read and remember. You will often see this format on official documents, payslips, and letters from HMRC.
  • Without Spaces: AB123456C — This format is also acceptable and is often used in computer systems, online forms, and databases.
  • With a Slash or Hyphen: Sometimes you might see AB/12 34 56 C or AB-12-34-56-C, but these are less common. The standard format with spaces is the preferred way to write a National Insurance Number.

On official documents like your P60, P45, or payslip, you will usually see your National Insurance Number written with spaces, like AB 12 34 56 C.

11. National Insurance Number Letters and Numbers

The National Insurance Number letters and numbers follow specific rules. Here is a detailed breakdown:

Letters at the Start

The first two letters of your National Insurance Number are always letters. These letters are usually chosen from a specific pool of allowed characters.

Allowed first letters: A, B, C, E, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, P, R, S, T, W, X, Y, Z

Disallowed first letters: D, F, I, Q, U, V

Allowed second letters: A, B, C, E, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, P, R, S, T, W, X, Y, Z

Disallowed second letters: D, F, I, O, Q, U, V

The Six Numbers

The six numbers can be any combination from 000000 to 999999. There are no restrictions on which numbers can appear.

The Final Letter

The final letter must be one of: A, B, C, or D.

Why These Rules Exist

These rules were established to ensure consistency across all National Insurance Numbers, prevent confusion with other identifiers, allow for easy validation of the number, and enable HMRC and employers to identify valid numbers quickly.

13. How to Identify a National Insurance Number

How to identify a National Insurance Number is a useful skill. Here are the key identifiers:

Visual Clues

  • Format: Two letters, six numbers, one letter
  • Spaces: Usually written with spaces: AB 12 34 56 C
  • Length: Always 9 characters
  • Final Letter: Always A, B, C, or D
  • First Letters: Follow the allowed letter rules

Where You Will Find It

  • Payslips: Usually at the top or bottom of the page
  • P60: Section 1, at the top of the form
  • P45: Part 1, at the top of the form
  • Personal Tax Account: Dashboard or profile section
  • HMRC Letters: Always included on correspondence
  • Benefit Letters: Included in all official communications

What It Is Not

  • It is not a passport number
  • It is not a driving licence number
  • It is not your date of birth
  • It is not your National Insurance contributions (those are separate)

How to Spot a Fake

  • The format will be wrong (too many or too few characters)
  • The final letter will not be A, B, C, or D
  • The first letters will be disallowed letters
  • The number will not be 9 characters

14. Where Can I Find My National Insurance Number?

Where can I find my National Insurance Number is a question many people ask. Here are all the places you can find it:

  • Your Payslips: Every payslip you receive from your employer will include your National Insurance Number. It is usually located near the top of the payslip, clearly labelled "NI Number" or "National Insurance Number."
  • Your P60: Your P60 is the annual tax summary you receive from your employer at the end of the tax year (5 April). It always includes your National Insurance Number at the top of the form.
  • Your P45: If you have left a job, your P45 will include your National Insurance Number. This is an important document for your tax records.
  • Personal Tax Account: You can find your National Insurance Number in your online Personal Tax Account on the GOV.UK website. This is one of the quickest ways to find it.
  • The HMRC App: Download the free HMRC app, and you can view your National Insurance Number instantly.
  • Letters from HMRC: Any official correspondence from HMRC will include your National Insurance Number — usually at the top of the letter.
  • Benefit Letters: Letters from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) regarding benefits will also include your National Insurance Number.
  • Your BRP (for non-UK citizens): If you have a Biometric Residence Permit (BRP), your National Insurance Number may be printed on the back of it.
  • Form CA5403: If you cannot find your number anywhere, you can complete form CA5403 ("Get your National Insurance number by post") on GOV.UK.

On a P45

Your P45 is issued when you leave a job. Your National Insurance Number appears at the top:

P45 - Leaving Certificate

Employee Name: John Smith

National Insurance Number: AB 12 34 56 C

Leaving Date: 15/06/2026

Why You Need to Check

It is important to check that your National Insurance Number is correct on these documents. If there is an error, your tax and contributions may not be recorded correctly, which could affect your State Pension and benefit entitlement.

A UK National Insurance Number always looks like this: AB 12 34 56 C. It consists of two letters at the beginning, six numbers in the middle, and one final letter (A, B, C, or D).
