1. Don't Panic — Your Number Doesn't Change

If you've lost your National Insurance Number, the good news is that your number stays the same for life — you just need to track down where it's written down, or request a confirmation letter. Losing the physical letter is common and doesn't affect your record with HMRC in any way.

If you've never actually been issued a number and think you may not have one at all, it's worth first checking the National Insurance Number eligibility criteria and reading how to check if you already have a National Insurance Number before assuming it's lost.

2. Places to Check Before Requesting a Replacement

Before contacting HMRC, it's worth checking a few obvious places, since your number is likely printed on documents you already have:

  • Payslips — most employers print your NI Number on every payslip
  • P60 or P45 — annual and end-of-employment tax summaries
  • Your Personal Tax Account — accessible online via your Government Gateway login
  • Old benefit or pension letters — from HMRC or the Department for Work and Pensions
  • Your Biometric Residence Permit — some BRPs display the number

3. How to Request a Replacement Letter

If none of the above turn up your number, you can request a confirmation letter from HMRC. This letter is not a new National Insurance Number, but a written confirmation of the number you already hold.

  1. Log in to (or create) your Personal Tax Account on GOV.UK
  2. Use the "find my National Insurance Number" service if available to you
  3. If you can't access it online, call HMRC's National Insurance helpline
  4. Alternatively, request support through NI NO's replacement application service

5. How Long a Replacement Letter Takes

A postal confirmation letter from HMRC typically takes around 15 working days to arrive once requested. If you need proof sooner, digital access through your Personal Tax Account is usually the fastest route, since it displays your number immediately once you're logged in.

Good to know: Your employer can often begin processing your payroll using emergency tax codes even before your confirmation letter arrives, so a lost number rarely needs to hold up starting work.

6. Protecting Your National Insurance Number

  • Keep a digital copy stored securely, such as a password-protected note
  • Avoid sharing your number over the phone unless you're certain who you're speaking to
  • Update your address with HMRC whenever you move, so future letters don't go missing
  • Consider updating your details through NI NO's update details service if your circumstances have changed

For further consumer protection advice on handling personal financial documents, Citizens Advice is a useful independent resource.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if I lose my National Insurance Number? +
First check old payslips, P60s or your Personal Tax Account, since your number is likely printed there. If you still can't find it, request a confirmation letter from HMRC.
Does my National Insurance Number change if I lose it? +
No, your National Insurance Number stays the same for life. Losing the letter does not affect the number itself.
How long does a replacement letter take to arrive? +
A postal confirmation letter typically takes around 15 working days to arrive after being requested.
Can I find my National Insurance Number online? +
Yes, if you have a Personal Tax Account with a Government Gateway login, your number is usually displayed there instantly.
Can my employer help me find my number? +
Yes, your current or previous employer may have your National Insurance Number on file from your payslips or employment records.
What if I've never received a National Insurance Number letter? +
You may already have a number even without the letter; check your Personal Tax Account or read our guide on checking if you already have a number.
Is there a fee to request a replacement letter? +
Requesting a confirmation letter directly from HMRC does not typically involve a government charge.
Can I start work without finding my old number? +
Yes, your employer can usually begin payroll using temporary arrangements while you locate or confirm your number.
Who can I contact for help finding my number? +
You can contact HMRC directly, or use a support service such as NI NO to help track it down.
